12 week plan · 2026 Oct 19 to 2027 Jan 10

30 Min Pivot Study Plan

The goal: a strategy that you trade by hand. Buy market leaders at low risk with a 30 minute pivot when they pull back to support, and hold the winners for a long ride. A low win rate is acceptable: a few big winners make the expectancy for each trade positive. In 12 weeks, first register the definitions and your predictions. Then measure the expectancy of the strategy from the raw records, test the entry against a placebo, compare ways to ride, and examine each of your trades. Last, one blind test measures the value of your eye when it chooses trades. The Predictions tab lists each registered prediction and if it held.

Goal

Find a strategy that you can follow: buy market leaders at low risk with a 30 minute pivot when they pull back to support, and hold the winners for a long ride. Its edge is the payoff, not the win rate. Most trades are small losses, and a few trades make many times their risk. So a low win rate is acceptable, but after costs the expectancy for each trade must be above 0. The scanner only lists the stocks. Your eye decides if a setup is good, and you put in each order by hand. In 12 weeks (2027 Jan 10), you answer four questions with evidence that does not depend on you or Claude. One, the payoff: the expectancy, the win rate and the payoff ratio of the current rules for each trade, and which support levels give the money. Two, the entry and the stop: how much the 30 minute pivot beats random entries on the same stock and day with the same risk, and how many big winners the pattern low stop shakes out. Three, the ride: how much of a big move the current exit keeps, and if a different exit gives a higher expectancy. Four, your eye: how much better the trades that you take are than all the trades on the scanner's list, and where your eye is right and where it fails. Also, a review of each order that you put in shows where the money leaks. The strategy itself and at most 3 rules each get one test on a sealed set. The outputs are a one page strategy card and the predictions ledger. If the evidence shows that no such strategy exists, that is also an answer.

For each filled setup, calculate R with the current rules (see the Glossary), minus 2 ticks of slippage and the commission. The expectancy, the win rate and the payoff ratio come from these R values. Shakeouts and big winners are counted with the triple barrier label: which comes first, the stop, the big winner level (by a close) or 60 sessions. Results on used data are descriptive only. Only the reserved blind charts and the sealed set give a test.

6 week checkpoint (week 6, Nov 29). At that time you have the first three answers (the payoff, the entry, the ride) and the review of your trades. You can stop there and use them. If you continue, you do the blind test of your eye and the sealed test.

Routines

Each week

  • 10 minutes on Sunday: Read the card for next week. Check that the events it waits for occurred. If not, change the weeks by the rules in “If you fall behind”. Each week's hours include these 10 minutes.
  • Practice: Use only charts that were revealed and are not reserved, in learn mode with an immediate reveal. Write down the types that you often get wrong. The hit rate is for learning only. It is not a test.

Each month (first weekend, about 20 minutes)

  • Reconcile: your own trades of the month. Match R and dollars to the statement, and check if you followed the current rules. Record only. Do not use them to find rules.

Week 4Nov 9 to 15 · Answers 1 to 3; your own orders

Answer 1: the payoff (census of expectancy and shakeouts)

you: about 3.9 h5 sessionsWaits for:Training charts sealedThis week

GoalLearn the expectancy, the win rate and the payoff ratio of the current rules for each trade after costs, and which support levels give the money. Learn how many big winners shook you out first, and how they differ from real failures.

Why
This is the base of the whole strategy. If the expectancy of the current rules is already positive, the later steps only make it better. If it is not, you must know if the gap is in the entry, the stop, the ride or the choice of stocks. The shakeouts are counted here too. If many big winners shake you out, and they go only a little below the stop, the stop position is the most valuable question.
How
Calculate the R of each filled setup with the current rules, after costs. Then calculate the expectancy, the win rate, the payoff ratio, the right tail, the longest losing streak and the deepest drawdown, and split them by the type of support and by the strength of the leader. The right tail is large, so each expectancy has a 90% interval from a bootstrap by stock, and the report also shows what is left without the best 5 trades. Triple barrier labels split the shakeouts, the real failures and the rest. MAE is in ATR and in stop units. Setups with no fill (cancels and expiries) are reported apart. Each number is next to the number from the second program. The 20 shaken out winners and 20 real failures that you examine all come from revealed stocks that are not reserved.
How it helps the strategy
It gives the numbers of the strategy card a start point. It also decides if two rules are worth a registration in week 10: where the stop goes, and which support to trade. And it gives your eye a clear target: tell shakeouts from real failures at the pivot close.

Week 5Nov 16 to 22 · Answers 1 to 3; your own orders

Answers 2 and 3: the entry and the ride

you: about 4.1 h5 sessionsWaits for:Placebo registeredThis week

GoalLearn what the 30 minute pivot timing adds over random entries on the same stock and day. Learn how much of a big move the current exit keeps, and if a longer ride gives a higher expectancy.

Why
If random entries do as well as the pivot, the money comes from the stocks that you choose, not from the 30 minute timing. Then your time goes to which stocks to take and skip, not to the timing on the bars. A strategy that makes money from the payoff gets most of it from a few big winners. The current exit sells half early and holds 60 sessions at most, so it can cut off exactly that part. These years are used data, so these results are descriptive. To make one a test, put it into the sealed registration of week 10.
How
Run as registered: 1,000 random entries for each setup, with the same risk in ATR, the same exit and the same costs. Compare the pivot with the random entries in pairs, and read the pivot result against the placebo distribution. Apply the registered kill rule to the result. Do not change it now. For the ride, run each filled trade with the current exit and with each exit chosen in week 1, until the exit or Sep 22. Compare them in pairs: the expectancy, the payoff ratio, the right tail, the mean days held and the capture ratio. Trades still open use the close of Sep 22 and are reported apart.
How it helps the strategy
It decides the direction of the rules in week 10: the timing, the choice of stocks, or the exit.

Week 6Nov 23 to 29 · Answers 1 to 3; your own orders

Your own orders: where the money leaks

you: about 4.0 h4 sessionsNov 26 closedNov 27 early closeThis week

GoalLearn the step where your money leaks: the decision, the delay, the fill or the size. Count your stops on stocks that later became big winners.

Why
You put in each order of this strategy by hand. Your live trades made money in R but lost money in dollars. A good expectancy in the research must still pass through your hands before money reaches the account. Implementation shortfall splits the gap, so you know if the fix is in the choice of stocks, the execution or the size.
How
Four parts: the decision (the trades that you took or skipped), the delay (from the alert to the order), the fill (slippage and commission) and the size (the dollars for each R against the plan). Each part needs its own record. If the record is missing, that part is marked “unknown”. The decision and the delay need the alert time and the order time, and the webapp has order records only from Sep 16. The size uses the entry, the first stop and the shares of each position, which the webapp keeps from Apr 15. The fill needs the order price. Before Sep 16 there is no record of it, so it can only be rebuilt from the rule (1 tick above the pivot high). That part is marked “rebuilt”, not your recorded plan. Trades from Sep 23 are only reconciled and checked for compliance. They do not give rules. You decide the fixes. Claude does not change a live setting and does not put in orders for you.
How it helps the strategy
If the leak is in the size or the execution, a fix there is worth more than a new rule. The count of your own shakeouts gives the stop rule of week 10 its weight in live trading.

Freeze the model of your scores; register the eye test

you: about 2.8 h5 sessionsThis week

GoalBefore the blind test, freeze and register the model and the test.

Why
To know if the model does better than you, both must work on the same charts that neither saw. The model must be frozen first, or the blind results can tune it. The lens model also compares with what you wrote in week 1. What you think you weigh is often not what you really weigh.
How
The model uses only the cues that code can calculate, and learns your 1 to 5 score and your take or skip on the training charts (logistic regression, cross validated). Freeze it and record its fingerprint. The registration states how the blind charts are drawn from the reserved stocks (layered by outcome, so that there are enough winners and shakeouts, with the weights written down), the number of hidden repeats, the terms of the lens model, the ROC and AUC, the winner rate at each score level, how the model and you are compared (the paired AUC difference, with a bootstrap by stock), and how the value of your choice is calculated (the paired difference in expectancy between your takes and all charts).
How it helps the strategy
You always decide take or skip. If the model does better, it is your second opinion: when you want a trade that the model scores low, stop and check the list first. If you do better, your eye sees something that the model cannot learn, and the strategy card must state what it is.

If you fall behind

  • Scoring, a registration or a seal falls behind: Do not make a session longer (45 minutes at most) and do not skip. Move the block one week. The later weeks move with it. Do not cut content to be on time, and do not read the sealed data early.
  • The missing 1 minute data is not complete: the census still gives the triple barrier labels (the big winner level is by a close, so daily bars give the order, except on the fill day). The time of day, the intraday recovery and the order on the fill day come when the data is complete. The report states which numbers are still missing.
  • The power report needs more charts: the training charts can start in week 2, and week 8 can grow to the cap of 300 minutes. The blind charts cannot start before the freeze in week 7.
  • IBKR has no ticks as far back as 2024: the census uses only the 1 minute data. It states how many trades have an unclear order of events in the minute when the price touched the stop.
  • The power is too low: do not run that test, and record the prediction as “too little data”. Do not add data or change the measure to make it work.
  • The model does not match you: freeze and compare it anyway. Then the result shows that your eye sees what the model cannot, or that neither of you can see it.
  • Too many trades are still open in the ride comparison (more than one third): compare only the first 60 sessions, and record the longer exits as “too little data”. Do not guess the end of the open trades.

Glossary

TermMeaning
30 minute pivotOn the 30 minute chart, after 3 or more down bars in a row, the first up bar that closes in its top third (the pivot bar). Put a buy stop order 1 tick above its high. Put the stop 1 tick below the low of the pattern. The order stays until the close of the next session. If the price goes below the pattern low before the fill, cancel the order.
ScannerThe program that you use live. After each 30 minute bar, it lists the stocks that meet the Strict Pivot rule. It only gives you the list. Your eye decides take or skip. This plan does not change it.
Strict PivotThe pivot that the scanner accepts now. The stock is on the scanner's list: relative strength in the top 20%, an up trend, and a prior close within 10% of the highest close of the last 52 weeks. The prior close is not too far above the 21 day exponential average. The pullback depth is at least 0.4 daily ATR. The stop distance is less than 60% of the average daily range of the last 20 days. The census uses this version.
Relative strength percentileAt the prior close, the rank of a stock's weighted 12 month return (recent months count more) in that day's liquid pool. The pool is US listed common stock and depositary receipts of foreign companies, with a price above $10 and an average daily dollar volume above $20 million over 50 days.
Market leaderIn this plan, a market leader is a stock on the scanner's list: relative strength in the top 20%, an up trend, and a prior close within 10% of the highest close of the last 52 weeks. In week 1 you can set a stricter group (recommended: the top 10%). The census reports it apart.
Support levelA price where a pullback can stop. Set the types in week 1. Three are recommended: the 21 day exponential average, the 50 day simple average, and the breakout level of the prior base. If the pattern low is within 0.5 ATR14 of one of them, the setup is a pullback to that support. If it is near none, record “no support”. Code calculates the averages. The breakout level comes from a registered rule.
ATR14The average true range of the last 14 days. It shows the normal daily move of the stock.
RThe profit or loss of a trade, divided by the planned risk (the distance from entry to stop, times the shares). A gain of 3R is three times the planned risk.
ExpectancyThe mean R for each filled trade, after costs: the win rate times the mean win, minus the loss rate times the mean loss (in R). The strategy makes money over time only if it is above 0.
Win rate and payoff ratioThe win rate is the share of filled trades with R above 0 after costs. The payoff ratio is the mean win divided by the mean loss, in R. For example, a win rate of 30%, a payoff ratio of 4 and a mean loss of 1R: the expectancy is 0.3 times 4 minus 0.7 times 1, which is 0.5R.
Right tailThe share of the total R that comes from the best 10% of filled trades. For a strategy that makes money from the payoff, this share is high. So if you miss or sell early a few big winners, the expectancy falls a lot.
Losing streak and drawdownPut all filled trades in time order. The losing streak is the most losses in a row. The drawdown is the largest fall of the total R from its high. A strategy with a low win rate always has long losing streaks. If you know their length first, you can stay with the strategy.
Stop unitThe distance from the entry to the stop. Use it to measure a move: 0.5 stop units below the stop is half of that distance more.
Big winnerWithin the pivot session and the next 60 sessions, the highest close is at least N ATR14 above the close of the pivot bar. Set N in week 1; 10 is recommended. The power simulation assumes that about 15% of Strict Pivots are big winners.
ShakeoutA filled trade hits the pattern low stop first, and the stock still closes at or above the big winner level within 60 sessions.
Real failureA filled trade hits the stop, and the stock does not close at or above the big winner level within 60 sessions.
No fill: cancel or expiryA buy stop that does not fill ends in one of two ways. A cancel: the price goes below the pattern low before a fill. An expiry: no fill and no cancel by the close of the next session. Neither is a trade. Report the two apart, and also show if they later became big winners.
Triple barrier labelFor each filled trade, find which of three barriers comes first: the stop, the big winner level (by a close) or 60 sessions. Split the trades that hit the stop first into shakeouts and real failures. The method comes from Marcos López de Prado (2018).
MAE and MFEMaximum adverse excursion and maximum favorable excursion: how far a trade went against you and for you before the exit, in ATR and in stop units. They come from John Sweeney.
ExitThe rule that decides when to sell. The current exit is in “Current rules”. In week 1, choose at most 2 exits that hold longer. Week 5 compares them on the same filled trades.
Capture ratioThe R at the exit of a trade, divided by the R at the highest close in the 60 sessions after the pivot: how much of the move the exit kept. An exit that holds longer can go above 100%.
CensusA count of one kind of event in all the setups, not in a few chosen charts.
Research dataDaily bars from 2024 Jan 2 to 2026 Sep 22, and 1 minute bars from 2024 Mar 25. Most of the 1 minute data is a sample: 48 stock months each month, drawn with a fixed seed. So Strict Pivots can be found only in those stock months. Sessions outside the sample need a download that you approve.
Survivors onlyThe data has only stocks that still trade today. Stocks that stopped trading are not in it. A winner rate from such data can be too high, so the conclusions apply only to surviving stocks.
Used dataData that was already used to look for patterns and set rules. All of 2024 to 2026 is used data. Results on it are descriptive. They are not a test.
Sealed setData that is locked and used only for one registered test. There are two sets now. 2020 to 2023 is kept for another eye test, and this plan does not use it. Until your scores for that test are sealed, you see no stock names and no row for each stock from those years. Nobody has read 2018 to 2019. It is read one time, and you see only the aggregates.
Forward periodSessions from 2026 Sep 23. They are kept for registered live tests. Do not use them to look for patterns. Your trades in this period are used only to reconcile and to check compliance.
Placebo entryA fake entry on the same stock on the same day: a random 30 minute bar that can take an entry, with the same risk in ATR and the same exit, repeated many times. Register first which bars can take an entry, what is known at that time, the order rules and the costs. The results of these random entries make the conditional placebo distribution.
Shuffled scoresShuffle your scores across the charts at random, many times, to see what chance gives. Read your real result against this distribution (a permutation test).
Meta labelingThe pivot gives the trade. A second step decides only take or skip, and the size. Here, the second step is your eye. A small model is only a benchmark. It comes from Marcos López de Prado.
CueOne feature that you use to judge a chart, for example the pullback depth, the volume dry up or the distance to the 21 day average. In week 2, write a list of at most 12 cues. Mark which ones code can calculate and which are eye only.
Lens modelIt splits the accuracy of your eye into three parts: how well you know the cues, how consistent you are, and how predictable the setups are. It comes from Egon Brunswik.
Model of your scoresA model that learns your scores on the training charts from the cues that code can calculate (judgmental bootstrapping, Lewis Goldberg, 1970). It never has a bad day, so it often does better than the person. Freeze it in week 7 and compare it with you only on the blind charts. It is a benchmark and a second opinion. It does not decide for you.
Training chartsCharts from the stocks that are not reserved. You score them in week 3. They train the model of your scores. After the seal, you see their outcomes and learn from them.
Reserved blind chartsA share of the stocks, reserved in week 1 with a fixed seed. Only setups before 2026 Apr 14 (your first live fill) count. They are in no output of any step, and you see none of them before the blind test.
Hidden repeatThe same chart occurs two times in a batch, and you do not know which charts repeat. When the two scores agree more, your eye is more consistent.
Weighted kappaHow well two sets of scores agree: 0 is chance agreement, 1 is full agreement.
FamiliarityFill it for each chart: unfamiliar, maybe seen, or recognized. You can know a recognized chart from memory, not from your eye, so we count those charts apart.
Balanced accuracyFor a yes or no call, the mean of the correct share among the yes cases and the correct share among the no cases: 0.5 is a guess, 1 is all correct.
ROC curve and AUCUse each of your score levels from 1 to 5 as a threshold, and plot the winners caught against the non winners caught. AUC is the area under the curve: 0.5 is a guess, 1 is all correct.
Winner rate at each score levelThe share of big winners among your charts with each score from 1 to 5. Your scores are useful only if the share goes up with the score (this plan calls it calibration).
Value of your choiceThe expectancy after costs of the trades that you take, minus the expectancy of all the trades on the scanner's list. Your eye adds money only if it is above 0.
Implementation shortfallThe difference between the planned result and the real dollars in the account, split into four parts: the decision (which trades you took or skipped), the delay, the fill (slippage and commission) and the size. It comes from André Perold (1988).
ReconciliationChange the R of each trade in the research into dollars, and match them to the dollars in the broker statement, trade by trade. State the cause of each difference.
RegistrationA file that you write before a test, have reviewed and commit. It states the data, the measures, the prediction and the rule for “held”. Its sha256 fingerprint goes into the public repository with an outside time stamp. A later change needs a written amendment before any data is read, with a review and your approval.
Kill ruleA result, written first, that ends one line of research. For example: if the pivot does not beat the placebo, stop the work on the 30 minute timing.
Smallest gain worth a changeThe smallest gain after costs, for each filled trade, that a new rule must give over the current rules to be worth a change. Write it in week 1. Recommended: 0.10R more, with the lower bound of the 90% interval above 0.
PowerThe chance that a test can see the smallest gain, with the setups and winners in the data. Calculate it by simulation, with a heavy tailed R (a few trades make many times their risk), not a normal one. The target is 80%. If it is lower, do not run that test, and record “too little data”.
Bootstrap intervalResample the data by stock many times, and see how far the result moves. This plan uses a 90% interval.
Holm correctionA correction that limits false positives when you do several tests at the same time. The false positive rate of all the tests together stays at or below a number that you set first.
Second programA program that a separate Claude agent writes from the written specification only, in a new research folder. It does not see the code of the first program. The main numbers must agree with the first program.
sha256 fingerprintA string calculated from the content of a file. If one character changes, the fingerprint changes. Write the fingerprint into a dated record that you can trust. Later, calculate it again and compare. This proves that the file did not change after that date.
Outside time stampA record that a third party keeps, which proves that a fingerprint existed on a date. Examples: an OpenTimestamps proof, or a snapshot of the public page in the Internet Archive. You can change a commit date. You cannot change an outside time stamp.
Seal and revealAfter a batch, record the sha256 fingerprint of the scores file in a dated note (seal). After that, any change shows. Only after the seal do you see what each chart did next (reveal).
CommitA commit saves a snapshot of the files with a date. Compare the files with this snapshot to find changes.
Current rules (the baseline)Entry: a buy stop 1 tick above the pivot high. Stop: 1 tick below the pattern low. Do not move the stop after the entry. Size: risk 0.2% of the account for each trade when the pullback depth is at least 0.7 daily ATR, 0.1% from 0.4 to 0.7, and no trade below 0.4. Total risk is 3% at most. After an 8% drawdown from the account high, cut the risk for each trade by half. Restore the full risk only at a new account high. Exit: when a close is 3 ATR above the 21 day exponential average, sell half at the next open. From the second session, sell the rest at the next open after a close below the 10 day exponential average. Hold for 60 sessions at most. 3 new stocks each day at most.
Tick and slippageA tick is the smallest step of a price, usually $0.01 for US stocks. Slippage is the difference between the order price and the fill price.
Strategy cardThe one page output of week 12: which stocks, which pullback, the entry, the stop, the ride, the size, and the take or skip list for your eye, with the expectancy, the win rate, the payoff ratio and the longest losing streak. Each line shows if it comes from a test or from a description.
Codex and advisorTwo independent AI reviewers. Each registration needs their review first. Codex reads only, and never reads the data.
