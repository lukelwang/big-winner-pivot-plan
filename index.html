14 week plan

30 Min Pivot Study Plan

In 14 weeks you make two things. First, a model book. Each time a big winner of 2020 to 2026 pulled back to support, you mark the 30 minute bar where you would buy and where the stop goes. Code shows if the trade was shaken out and how many R it made. Then the entry of the model book becomes a rule. The rule finds all the setups that look the same, if they rose or not, and gives the expectancy year by year. Last, you write a one page strategy card and check it one time on data that nobody has seen. The Predictions tab lists the estimates that you write first, and if they were right.

Goal

Find a strategy that you can follow: buy market leaders at low risk with a 30 minute pivot when they pull back to support, and hold the winners for a long ride. Its edge must come from the payoff, not the win rate. Most trades are small losses, and a few trades make many times their risk. So a low win rate is acceptable, but after costs the expectancy for each trade must be above 0. As William O'Neil did, start with the winners. Make a model book of the 30 biggest moves of each year from 2020 to 2026. You look at the whole chart, and at each pullback to support you mark the 30 minute bar where you would buy, with the stop. Code calculates if the trade was shaken out and how many R it made. Where the trader that you follow posted the same move, his entry goes next to yours. But winners alone show only that the entry existed. They do not show if it makes money. So the entry of the model book becomes a rule. The rule finds all the setups of the same years that look the same, if they rose or not. It gives the win rate, the payoff ratio and the expectancy, year by year. The outputs are a model book and a one page strategy card. Last, the strategy card gets one check on 2018 to 2019, a set that nobody has seen. If the evidence shows that no such strategy exists, that is also an answer.

For each entry and each setup that looks the same, calculate R with a stop and an exit that are written first, minus 2 ticks of slippage and the commission. The expectancy, the win rate and the payoff ratio come from these R values. A second version uses double costs. There are three stops (the pattern low, the pivot bar low and your own stop) and two exits. All of them are written before you start to mark. Shakeouts and big winners are counted with the triple barrier label.

Week 7 checkpoint. At that time the model book is complete, and each entry has its numbers. You can stop there and use it. If you continue, it becomes a strategy card with an expectancy.

Routines

Each week

  • 10 minutes on Sunday: Read the card for next week. Check that the events it waits for occurred. If not, change the weeks by the rules in “If you fall behind”. Each week's hours include these 10 minutes.
  • 45 minutes at most each session: Do the marking and the pairs in several sessions, and stop when you are tired. If you mark too much at one time, the later marks become careless.

Every 4 weeks (about 20 minutes)

  • Reconcile (weeks 4, 8 and 12): your own trades of the last 4 weeks. Match R and dollars to the statement, and check if you followed the current rules. Record only. Do not use them to find rules.

Week 1Fix the measures, list the winners

Fix the measures, list the winners

you: about 2.4 h

GoalBefore you mark any move, write the measures, the three stops, the two exits and your predictions. Commit the change for 2020 to 2023. List the 30 biggest moves of each year from 2020 to 2026. Build the marking page of the model book.

Why
Two things fool you most when you study winners: the stop and the R. When the outcome is in view, you can always put the stop just below the low that held later. You can always take the high as an R that you could get. So the stops and the exits are fixed before you see the first chart. There are two written stops, and your own stop is a column of its own. R comes only from the two written exits. For 2020 to 2023, a written change must be committed first, before the scan for big moves and before you see the stock names of those years.
How
Claude drafts the measures and you correct them: the leaders, the big moves, the supports and how near is near, the three stops, the two exits, the costs and the cut for “most years”. Claude writes the change, and it is committed after your approval. Then the daily bars give all big moves of the leaders from 2020 to 2026. Each year, the 30 biggest are taken, with 2 at most for each stock in a year. Claude counts the missing 30 minute data of these moves and of all leader months. The marking page shows the weekly, daily and 30 minute charts together, with the symbol and the dates, and no bar before 2020.
How it helps the strategy
Write the numbers that you expect first. Weeks 7, 11 and 12 compare them with the real numbers. You learn where your feel for this setup is right and where it is off.

  • Check that the download of 2018 to 2019 is complete. Do not open it.
  • Build the marking page: the three charts together; click a bar to mark an entry, choose the support, click the stop, give a score and write a reason. Marks can only be added. The numbers of a move come only after its marks are saved. If the 5 tries take more than 5 minutes a move on average, change the batches before week 2.

Week 2Make the model book

Mark the model book (batch 1)

you: about 3.5 hWaits for: Change for 2020 to 2023 committed, Measures and predictions committed

GoalMark about 40 big moves. At each pullback to support, mark the 30 minute bar where you would buy, the support, your stop, a score and one line of reason.

Why
The value of a model book is in the count. When one setup comes again and again in many stocks and many years, your eye can learn it, and you can see when it fails. The crash and rise of 2020, the boom of 2021, the bear market of 2022 and the rise of 2023 to 2026 all need enough examples.
How
The moves come in a random order, and each batch mixes all years. For each move, first see the weekly and daily charts of the whole move, then the 30 minute chart. At each pullback to support, click the bar where you would buy and choose the support. Click your stop, give a score from 1 to 5 and write one line of reason. Do not change a mark. Each session is 45 minutes at most. The page shows the numbers from code and the entry of the trader that you follow only after you save the marks. Mark first, then see.
How it helps the strategy
These entries are the source of the entry rule in week 8, and the examples for each reason on the eye list of the strategy card.

  • After your approval, download the missing 30 minute data at night and on weekends: the model book moves first, then all leader months.

Week 7Make the model book

Read the model book

you: about 2.4 hWaits for: Model book marked

GoalRead the whole model book. Learn how many big winners a 30 minute pivot caught, which stop kept them, and how much each of the two exits made. Compare with the entries of the trader that you follow. Write what the winners share.

Why
This is the question that this study starts from. When big winners pulled back to support, could a 30 minute pivot get you in, not shaken out, with a very high R? The model book answers it page by page. But it answers only for the big moves. So these numbers show if the entry can catch winners. They do not show if it makes money.
How
Claude makes the summary: the shakeout share with each stop, MAE and MFE, and the R and the days of the two exits, split by support, year and market state. It also shows how many of your entries the scanner's Strict Pivot alerts, and how your entries and stops differ from those of the trader that you follow. You read it, then write what the winners share, at most 8 lines. Then Claude picks 10 entries at random, and you check the numbers from code against their charts.
How it helps the strategy
Your lines are the draft of the entry rule in week 8. The stop that keeps the winners is the start point to choose the stop in week 11.

Week 8The entry rule and the expectancy

Turn the model book into an entry rule

you: about 2.2 h

GoalWrite your entries as a rule that code can find, and commit it with the week 8 predictions. Only after the commit are all setups counted.

Why
To learn if the entry makes money, you must apply it to the setups that did not rise later. So first there must be a rule that finds the setups that look the same in all leaders. The rule is written and committed before any summary, so that it does not move toward a good result without your notice.
How
Claude first groups your entries by the support, the shape of the pullback and the shape of the pivot bar, then writes a draft rule. It shows how many of your entries the draft finds again, and which bars in the same moves it adds that you did not mark. You correct it until you agree. The stop is the pattern low for now. Week 11 compares it with the pivot bar low.
How it helps the strategy
This rule is the base of weeks 9 to 14, and the “entry” line of the strategy card.

Week 9The entry rule and the expectancy

All setups that look the same: the expectancy

you: about 2.7 hWaits for: Entry rule and predictions committed, 30 minute data of all leader months downloaded

GoalLearn if the entry makes money after costs on all the setups that look the same, and how much in each year. Learn if the 30 minute pivot does better or worse than the simple entry.

Why
This is the expectancy that you want. A low win rate is acceptable, but you must know how low, how high the payoff ratio is, and what is left after costs. The 7 years have rises and falls. Year by year, you see if it works in all of them or only by luck in one or two. The comparison with the simple entry shows if the money comes from the 30 minute timing or only from the choice of stock and support.
How
The rule finds each setup in all leader months of 2020 to 2026. Each one gets the written order method, the pattern low stop and the current exit, and R after costs. There is no daily cap and no total risk limit. The expectancy, the win rate, the payoff ratio, the right tail, the longest losing streak and the deepest drawdown are split by year and by support, with a 90% interval. Also the shares of shakeouts and real failures, and the comparison with the simple entry year by year. After you read it, you examine 20 shaken out winners and 20 real failures.
How it helps the strategy
It gives the numbers of the strategy card a start point. It also shows where weeks 10 and 11 must tighten: which year and which support lose money, and if shakeouts and real failures differ in a way that you can see at the pivot close.

Claude delivers

Week 10The entry rule and the expectancy

Winners and lookalike failures side by side

you: about 2.7 hWaits for: All setups that look the same counted

GoalExamine about 40 pairs. Each pair is a winner with a lookalike failure of the same week that did not rise. Write the differences that you can see as a cue list.

Why
Only the features that the winners have and the lookalike failures seldom have can tell a winner. In a pair of the same week, the market and the time are the same, so the differences that are left can come from the setup itself.
How
Claude draws the pairs at random with a fixed seed. For each pair: a random week, then a random filled winner and a random filled lookalike failure with the same support in that week. There are 6 pairs at most from one year. For each pair you see the whole chart, first shown only up to the pivot close, then with the later path. You write how each pair differs. Then you make a list of at most 12 cues, each marked “code can calculate” or “eye only”.
How it helps the strategy
The list is the material to tighten the rule in week 11, and the source of the eye list of the strategy card.

Week 11Tighten: cues, stop, exit

Tighten: cues and the stop

you: about 1.8 h

GoalAdd at most 3 cues from the list to the entry rule, and each must pass the “most years” test. Choose the stop.

Why
The rule can change, so something must stop luck. A cue that makes the expectancy better only in the total is often the luck of one or two years. It is more likely to be real if it is better in most of the 7 years. This test does not stop all luck, so the count of cues stays small, and the sealed set gives one last check. The stop is the other half of the question that this study starts from. A near stop gives a larger R, but a shakeout is more likely. What counts is the expectancy of the two together.
How
Claude adds each cue that code can calculate to the rule alone, and shows the change of the expectancy in each of the 7 years. You choose at most 3 that pass. Then they are added together, and together they must also pass. The eye only cues do not go into the rule. On the eye list, each one is marked “not tested”, and the numbers on the card do not use it. The two written stops are each calculated on the tightened rule: the expectancy, the shakeout share and the longest losing streak. The pivot bar low takes the place of the pattern low only if it passes the “most years” test. Your own stop is only on the model book entries, so it is reported there, apart.
How it helps the strategy
The tightened rule and the chosen stop are the “entry” and “stop” lines of the strategy card.

Week 12Tighten: cues, stop, exit

The ride: the two exits

you: about 1.9 h

GoalCompare the two exits on the tightened rule, and choose the exit. Learn the longest losing streak and the deepest drawdown of the whole strategy.

Why
A strategy that makes money from the payoff gets most of it from a few big winners. The current exit sells half early and holds 60 sessions at most, so it can cut exactly that part. A strategy with a low win rate always has long losing streaks. If you know their length first, you can stay with the strategy.
How
Each fill runs with both exits. Compare the expectancy, the payoff ratio, the right tail, the mean days held and the capture ratio in pairs. The longer exit takes the place of the current exit only if it passes the “most years” test. Trades still open use the close of the last session before the forward period, and are reported apart. Then, with the chosen exit and the current size rules and limits, put all fills in time order. Calculate the longest losing streak and the deepest drawdown, and how far the account falls.
How it helps the strategy
The exit is the “ride” line of the strategy card. The losing streak and the drawdown set “when to stop and check”.

Week 13The strategy card and the last check

The strategy card draft and the last check

you: about 1.8 hWaits for: Download of 2018 to 2019 complete

GoalWrite the draft of the strategy card. Write the check on one page and commit it. Read the sealed set one time, and see only the aggregates.

Why
You have seen and used all of 2020 to 2026. Part of a good result on it comes from the changes that you made on the same data. Nobody has seen 2018 to 2019. One check on it shows if the strategy card still works in moves that it has not seen. Read it one time only: after a second read, it is not unseen any more.
How
You write the draft of the strategy card with Claude. Claude copies the rule, the stop and the exit of the card into the check page. The page states the data (only leader months with an up trend), what is calculated, the smallest count of fills and the pass condition. Recommended pass condition: for the two years together, the lower bound of the 90% interval of the expectancy is above 0. Before the read, you fill the last prediction slot. You approve the page, it is committed, and then Claude reads the set one time and reports the aggregates. If the expectancy of 2020 to 2026 after costs is not above 0 at the end of week 12, the card states “no edge found”, and the sealed set is not read.
How it helps the strategy
It decides if the strategy card says “passed the check” or “waits for live evidence”.

The last check (week 13)The sealed set: 2018 to 2019, only its leader months that also have an up trend.With the rule, the stop and the exit of the strategy card: the expectancy for each trade after costs, the win rate and the payoff ratio, for the two years together. If you fall behind

  • Marking falls behind: Do not make a session longer (45 minutes at most) and do not skip. Move this week and all later weeks by one week. Do not mark fewer moves to be on time, except by the 16 week rule below, and do not read the sealed set early.
  • The plan takes 16 weeks at most. Marking can use two more weeks at most. If that is not enough, you can cut the moves of each year from 30 to 25. No other step is cut. Keep the 25 biggest moves of each year. Marks on the other moves stay, but they are not in the summary.
  • The 30 minute data is not complete: mark the moves whose data is complete first. The counts of all setups wait for all leader months, and this week and the later weeks move.
  • A year has fewer than 30 big moves: take all that it has. Do not lower the big move level.
  • The order inside the fill bar is not clear (the entry and the stop in the same 30 minute bar): use the 1 minute data where it exists. If not, take the result that is worse for you, and report the count.
  • Too few setups that look the same (fewer than 100 fills in all 7 years for one support): describe that support only. It does not go into the main rule of the strategy card.
  • This order does not change. 1: the measures, the three stops, the two exits and your week 1 predictions, before you mark the first move. 2: the change for 2020 to 2023, before the scan for big moves of those years and before any winner is listed. 3: the commit of the entry rule and the week 8 predictions, before any summary of all setups. 4: the commit of the check page before the sealed set is read. 5: the sealed set is read one time, and you see only the aggregates. After the read, the rule, the stop and the exit on the card do not change.

Glossary

TermMeaning
Model bookThe method of William O'Neil: put the charts of past big winners together and find what they looked like before the rise. The model book of this plan has the 30 biggest moves of each year from 2020 to 2026, about 210 moves. In each move, you mark the 30 minute bar where you would buy at each pullback to support. You see the outcome in this step, so it teaches. It cannot give the expectancy.
Model book pageOne page for each big move. It shows the weekly, daily and 30 minute charts, with the symbol and the dates. It shows each entry that you marked, the three stop levels and your reason. It shows the numbers from code: shaken out or not, MAE and MFE, and the R and the days of each of the two exits. Where the trader that you follow posted the move, his entry comes onto the page after you mark it.
The trader that you followThe trader that you subscribe to, who often posts winner charts with the exact entry. His posts cover only about the last year, so they can be compared only with the moves of 2025 and 2026. He posts the charts that he chooses, not all his trades, so they cannot give a win rate.
Big moveA rise from a low: within 60 sessions, a close at least N ATR14 above it. The stock must be a leader in the month of the low, and the move belongs to the year of the low. Set N in week 1; 10 is recommended. Two moves of one stock do not overlap. Each year, sort the moves by their size in ATR14 and take the 30 biggest, with 2 at most for each stock in a year.
Big winnerWithin the pivot session and the next 60 sessions, the highest close is at least N ATR14 above the close of the pivot bar (the same N as for a big move). It splits the setups that look the same into winners and lookalike failures.
Market leaderA stock whose relative strength is in the top 10% of the liquid pool (RS 90 or more) on the first session of a month is a leader for that month. Set the cut in week 1; the top 10% is recommended.
Leader monthOne leader stock in one month. The download and the counts use it as the unit.
Stock monthOne stock in one month. The 30 minute data is downloaded by stock month.
Relative strength percentileAt the prior close, the rank of a stock's weighted 12 month return (recent months count more) in that day's liquid pool. The pool is US listed common stock and depositary receipts of foreign companies, with a price above $10 and an average daily dollar volume above $20 million over 50 days.
Up trendThe condition of the scanner's list: the prior close is above the 50 day average, the 50 day average is above the 200 day average, and the 200 day average is higher than 20 sessions before.
Support levelA price where a pullback can stop: the 21 day exponential average, the 50 day simple average, or the breakout level of the prior base. You can add others in week 1. Week 1 sets a first value for how near is near, and week 8 checks it against your marks. If the setup is near none, record “no support”. Code calculates the averages. The breakout level comes from a written rule.
Pullback depthThe distance from the high before the pullback to its lowest point, in daily ATR14.
30 minute pivotAfter a pullback, the bar on the 30 minute chart that you see as the turn and a buy (the pivot bar). Which bars count comes from your entries in the model book (week 8). The order method is fixed: a buy stop 1 tick above its high. The order stays until the close of the next session. If the price goes below the stop level in use before the fill, cancel the order.
Pattern lowThe first stop: the lowest price of the pullback, from the last swing high before the pivot (the highest bar with 3 bars on each side) to the pivot bar. The stop is 1 tick below it. The scanner's stop looks only at the string of red bars before the pivot, so the two are sometimes not the same.
Pivot bar lowThe second stop: the low of the pivot bar, with the stop 1 tick below it. It is nearer than the pattern low. So the same rise gives a larger R, but a shakeout is more likely. The trader that you follow often uses this stop.
Your stopThe third stop: the stop level that you click when you mark an entry. It exists only on your entries in the model book, so it is reported only there, apart. When the outcome is in view, it is easy to click just below the low that held later. So it does not set a rule. Only the two written stops set a rule.
The two exitsTwo sell rules that are written in week 1, before you start to mark. One is the current exit (see “Current rules”). One holds longer (recommended: after a close below the 21 day exponential average, sell all at the next open, and hold 120 sessions at most). R is calculated only with these two, not at the highest point, because a live trade cannot sell at the high.
Entry ruleOne rule in code that week 8 takes from your entries in the model book: the support, the shape of the pullback, the shape of the pivot bar, and the stop. It can have a branch for each support, and the expectancy is shown for each branch. It only finds all the setups that look the same. Weeks 11 and 12 can change it, but each change must pass the “most years” test.
Setups that look the sameEach setup that the entry rule finds in all leader months of 2020 to 2026, if it rose or not. The pivot session and the next 60 sessions must end before the forward period starts. In one pullback, only the first bar that the rule finds counts. A stock has no new setup while its trade is open. The expectancy, the win rate and the payoff ratio are calculated on all of them, not only on the winners.
Lookalike failuresThe setups that look the same but did not become big winners. If you look only at winners, you find many features that all winners have. Only the features that the winners have and the lookalike failures seldom have can tell a winner.
Simple entryThe entry used for comparison: in a leader month, buy at the close of the first day in each pullback that the stock reaches the same support, if a pivot comes later or not. That close and the next 60 sessions end before the forward period starts. The stop is 1 tick below the lowest price from the last swing high to that close. The exit is the same. Compare the two entries year by year, not in pairs. The difference between the two entries is what the 30 minute timing adds. It can be below 0.
Most yearsThe test that keeps luck out of the changes. A cue or a change stays only if it makes the expectancy better in at least 5 of the 7 years, and in the total. The stop and the exit also pass it: the pivot bar low takes the place of the pattern low, and the longer exit takes the place of the current exit, only if it passes. A year counts only if it has at least 30 fills before and after the change. 2026 is a part year. If one year does not count, the cut is 4 of the 6 that count. If two or more years do not count, the change does not pass. The test runs on the whole rule, not on each branch. Set the cut in week 1 (recommended: 5 of 7). It does not change after that.
CueOne feature that you use to judge a chart, for example the pullback depth, the volume dry up or the distance to the 21 day average. In week 10, make a list of at most 12 cues from your reasons and the pairs that you examined. Mark which ones code can calculate and which are eye only.
Market stateThe trend state of the Nasdaq 100 fund (QQQ) that the scanner sets after each close: green, yellow, amber or red. Code calculates it for each past session with the same rule. This plan uses it only to split the results.
ShakeoutA filled trade hits the stop first, and the stock still closes at or above the big winner level within 60 sessions. In the model book it is counted for each of the three stops. On all setups it is counted for the two written stops.
Real failureA filled trade hits the stop, and the stock does not close at or above the big winner level within 60 sessions.
No fill: cancel or expiryA buy stop that does not fill ends in one of two ways. A cancel: the price goes below the stop level in use before a fill. An expiry: no fill and no cancel by the close of the next session. Neither is a trade. Report the two apart, and also show if they later became big winners.
Triple barrier labelFor each filled trade, find which of three barriers comes first: the stop, the big winner level (by a close) or 60 sessions. Split the trades that hit the stop first into shakeouts and real failures. The method comes from Marcos López de Prado (2018).
MAE and MFEMaximum adverse excursion and maximum favorable excursion: how far a trade went against you and for you before the exit, in ATR and in stop units. They come from John Sweeney.
Stop unitThe distance from the entry to the stop. Use it to measure a move: 0.5 stop units below the stop is half of that distance more.
ATR14The average true range of the last 14 days. It shows the normal daily move of the stock. “Daily ATR” means the ATR14 of the daily bars.
RThe profit or loss of a trade, divided by the planned risk (the distance from entry to stop, times the shares). A gain of 3R is three times the planned risk.
ExpectancyThe mean R for each filled trade, after costs: the win rate times the mean win, minus the loss rate times the mean loss (in R). The strategy makes money over time only if it is above 0.
Win rate and payoff ratioThe win rate is the share of filled trades with R above 0 after costs. The payoff ratio is the mean win divided by the mean loss, in R. For example, a win rate of 30%, a payoff ratio of 4 and a mean loss of 1R: the expectancy is 0.3 times 4 minus 0.7 times 1, which is 0.5R.
Right tailThe share of the total R that comes from the best 10% of filled trades. For a strategy that makes money from the payoff, this share is high. So if you miss or sell early a few big winners, the expectancy falls a lot.
Losing streak and drawdownPut all filled trades in time order. The losing streak is the most losses in a row. The drawdown is the largest fall of the total R from its high. A strategy with a low win rate always has long losing streaks. If you know their length first, you can stay with the strategy.
Capture ratioThe R at the exit of a trade, divided by the R at the highest close in the 60 sessions after the pivot: how much of the move the exit kept. An exit that holds longer can go above 100%.
Bootstrap intervalResample the data by stock many times, and see how far the result moves. This plan uses a 90% interval.
Current rulesIn this plan the baseline stop is the pattern low, not the scanner's stop. Entry: a buy stop 1 tick above the pivot high. Stop: 1 tick below the scanner's stop level. Do not move the stop after the entry. Size: risk 0.2% of the account for each trade when the pullback depth is at least 0.7 daily ATR, 0.1% from 0.4 to 0.7, and no trade below 0.4. Total risk is 3% at most. After an 8% drawdown from the account high, cut the risk for each trade by half. Restore the full risk only at a new account high. Exit: when a close is 3 ATR above the 21 day exponential average, sell half at the next open. From the second session, sell the rest at the next open after a close below the 10 day exponential average. Hold for 60 sessions at most. 3 new stocks each day at most.
Tick and slippageA tick is the smallest step of a price, usually $0.01 for US stocks. Slippage is the difference between the order price and the fill price.
ScannerThe program that you use live. After each 30 minute bar, it lists the stocks that meet the Strict Pivot rule. It only gives you the list. Your eye decides take or skip. This plan does not change it.
Strict PivotThe pivot that the scanner accepts now. The stock is on the scanner's list: relative strength in the top 20%, an up trend, and a prior close within 10% of the highest close of the last 52 weeks. The prior close is not too far above the 21 day exponential average. The pullback depth is at least 0.4 daily ATR. The stop distance is less than 60% of the average daily range of the last 20 days. This plan compares it with your marks.
Research dataDaily and 30 minute data of 2020 to 2026. For 2020 to 2023, a written change must be committed first, before the scan for big moves and before any stock name is listed (week 1). The missing 30 minute stock months need a download that you approve. It runs only at night and on weekends. Each move and the 60 sessions after it must end before the forward period starts. The charts show no bar before 2020. Code uses daily bars before 2020 only to start the averages, ATR14, relative strength and base levels of 2020, and for the week 13 check. They are never shown.
Survivors onlyThe data has only stocks that still trade today. Stocks that stopped trading are not in it. So some failures are missing and the expectancy can be too high, more so in the early years. The conclusions apply only to surviving stocks, and the strategy card says so.
Sealed setThe 30 minute data of 2018 to 2019. Nobody has read it. It is read one time in the whole plan (week 13), and the check is written on one page and committed before the read. You see only the aggregates. Its download has the stock months of the scanner's list type (relative strength in the top 20%, an up trend). So the check uses only the leader months among them that also have an up trend, and the check page states this. The 2020 to 2026 numbers next to the check use the same up trend condition. The up trend is tested at the prior close of each setup, in both periods. The model book charts show no bar before 2020 to keep the set unseen.
Check pageOne page, written and committed before the sealed set is read: which entry rule, which stop and exit, what is calculated, and the pass condition. It does not change after the commit. After the read, it records only if the strategy passed. After the read, the rule, the stop and the exit on the card do not change. If one changes, the card states that the changed version has no check.
Waits for live evidenceWhat the strategy card says if the check does not pass or has too little data. You decide if you still use it at the screen. If you do, the risk for each trade does not go up. When your own trades (reconciled every 4 weeks) reach at least 100 fills, look at their expectancy.
Forward periodThe sessions after the scanner's live test started. They are kept for later tests, and this plan does not use them to look for patterns. Your trades in this period are used only to reconcile and as the live evidence.
Strategy cardThe one page output of week 14. It states which stocks, which pullback, the entry, the stop, the ride, the size, and the take or skip list for your eye. It gives the expectancy, the win rate, the payoff ratio, the right tail and the longest losing streak, each by year. It states if it passed the check on the sealed set.
ReconciliationChange the R of each of your own trades into dollars, and match them to the dollars in the broker statement, trade by trade. Check if you followed the rules, and state the cause of each difference. Record only. Do not use it to find rules.
CommitA commit saves a snapshot of the files with a date. Compare the files with this snapshot to find changes.
