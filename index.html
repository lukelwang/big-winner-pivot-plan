Big Winner Pivot Plan

One question in 12 weeks: how do you buy future big winners at low risk with a 30 minute pivot, and not get shaken out? First, register the definitions and your predictions. Then count the shakeouts from the raw records, test the pivot against a placebo, and examine each of your trades. Last, one blind test shows where your eye is right and where it fails. The Predictions tab lists each registered prediction and if it held.

Goal

In 12 weeks (2027 Jan 10), you know three things, with evidence that does not depend on you or Claude. One: how many big winners hit the pattern low stop first and shook you out, and what separates them from real failures at the pivot close. Two: if the 30 minute pivot beats random entries on the same stock and day, with the same risk. Three: where your eye is right and where it fails, and if a frozen model of your own scores does better. At most 3 rules each get one test on a sealed set. The outputs are a one page eye guide and the predictions ledger.

Each filled setup gets a triple barrier label (see the Glossary): which comes first, the stop, the big winner level (by a close) or 60 sessions. Calculate R with the current rules (see the Glossary), minus 2 ticks of slippage and the commission. Results on used data are descriptive only. Only the reserved blind charts and the sealed set give a test.

Routines

Each session (decision log, 10 to 15 minutes, from the day the log page is live, about Oct 21)

  • Before the open (06:00 to 06:25 PT): From the scanner's list of the day, mark up to 10 stocks that you want to own for this move. Rank them 1 to 10. Write one line of reason for each.
  • During the session: Write one row for each Strict Pivot alert. Record 1 to 5 for “will it become a big winner”, yes or no for “will it hit the stop first”, buy or not, and the decision (take, pass on the chart, no room, not seen). Record the reason (cues). After the model is frozen (from week 7), the page shows the model's score only after your score.
  • On time: Write the row before the first outcome of the order (a fill, a cancel below the pattern low before a fill, or the expiry). If you did not see the alert, write “not seen”. Do not add a score later. The page records the time of each row and the rule version at that time. Only a score written within 30 minutes of the alert (before the next bar closes) counts as a judgment at the pivot close. A later row keeps its decision, but it is marked “late” and stays out of the score analysis.
  • After the close: Do not look at a summary. Look only at your own trades.

Each week

  • 10 minutes on Sunday: Read the card for next week. Check that the events it waits for occurred. If not, change the weeks by the rules in “If you fall behind”. Each week's hours include these 10 minutes.
  • Practice: Use only charts that were revealed and are not reserved, in learn mode with an immediate reveal. Write down the types that you often get wrong. The hit rate is for learning only. It is not a test.

Each month (first weekend, about 20 minutes)

  • Compliance: Check the share of alerts that have a score. The target is 80% or more. If it is lower for two consecutive months, keep only the scores and the decision in the form. Also check the counts, and the R and dollars of your own trades.
  • Do not read: a grouped summary of the log. Calculate it only on the registered dates. In 2027 Apr, read only the stop trading criteria and compliance. The decision comes in 2028 Mar.

Week 6Nov 23 to 29 · Answers 5 to 7: shakeouts, placebo, your trades

Answer 7: your trades

you: about 3.5 h4 sessionsNov 26 closedNov 27 early closeThis week

GoalLearn the step where your money leaks: the decision, the delay, the fill or the size. Count your stops on stocks that later became big winners.

Why
Your live trades made money in R but lost money in dollars. A good rule in the research must still pass through your hands before money reaches the account. Implementation shortfall splits the gap, so you know if the fix is in the choice of stocks, the execution or the size.
How
Four parts: the decision (the trades that you took or skipped), the delay (from the alert to the order), the fill (slippage and commission) and the size (the dollars for each R against the plan). Each part needs its own record. If the record is missing, that part is marked “unknown”. The decision and the delay need the alert time and the order time, and the webapp has order records only from Sep 16. The size uses the entry, the first stop and the shares of each position, which the webapp keeps from Apr 15. The fill needs the order price. Before Sep 16 there is no record of it, so it can only be rebuilt from the rule (1 tick above the pivot high). That part is marked “rebuilt”, not your recorded plan. Trades from Sep 23 are only reconciled and checked for compliance. They do not give rules. You decide the fixes. Claude does not change a live setting.
How it helps the strategy
If the leak is in the size or the execution, a fix there is worth more than a new rule. The count of your own shakeouts gives the stop rule of week 10 its weight in live trading.

Lens modelIt splits the accuracy of your eye into three parts: how well you know the cues, how consistent you are, and how predictable the setups are. It comes from Egon Brunswik.
Model of your scoresA model that learns your scores on the training charts from the cues that code can calculate (judgmental bootstrapping, Lewis Goldberg, 1970). It never has a bad day, so it often does better than the person. Freeze it in week 7 and compare it with you only on the blind charts.
Training chartsCharts from the stocks that are not reserved. You score them in week 3. They train the model of your scores. After the seal, you see their outcomes and learn from them.
Reserved blind chartsA share of the stocks, reserved in week 1 with a fixed seed. Only setups before 2026 Apr 14 (your first live fill) count. They are in no output of any step, and you see none of them before the blind test.
Hidden repeatThe same chart occurs two times in a batch, and you do not know which charts repeat. When the two scores agree more, your eye is more consistent.
Weighted kappaHow well two sets of scores agree: 0 is chance agreement, 1 is full agreement.
FamiliarityFill it for each chart: unfamiliar, maybe seen, or recognized. You can know a recognized chart from memory, not from your eye, so we count those charts apart.
Balanced accuracyFor a yes or no call, the mean of the correct share among the yes cases and the correct share among the no cases: 0.5 is a guess, 1 is all correct.
ROC curve and AUCUse each of your score levels from 1 to 5 as a threshold, and plot the winners caught against the non winners caught. AUC is the area under the curve: 0.5 is a guess, 1 is all correct.
Winner rate at each score levelThe share of big winners among your charts with each score from 1 to 5. Your scores are useful only if the share goes up with the score (this plan calls it calibration).
Implementation shortfallThe difference between the planned result and the real dollars in the account, split into four parts: the decision (which trades you took or skipped), the delay, the fill (slippage and commission) and the size. It comes from André Perold (1988).
ReconciliationChange the R of each trade in the research into dollars, and match them to the dollars in the broker statement, trade by trade. State the cause of each difference.
RegistrationA file that you write before a test, have reviewed and commit. It states the data, the measures, the prediction and the rule for “held”. Its sha256 fingerprint goes into the public repository with an outside time stamp. A later change needs a written amendment before any data is read, with a review and your approval.
Kill ruleA result, written first, that ends one line of research. For example: if the pivot does not beat the placebo, stop the work on the 30 minute timing.
Smallest gain worth a changeThe smallest gain after costs, for each filled trade, that a new rule must give over the current rules to be worth a change. Write it in week 1. Recommended: 0.10R more, with the lower bound of the 90% interval above 0.
PowerThe chance that a test can see the smallest gain, with the setups and winners in the data. Calculate it by simulation. The target is 80%. If it is lower, do not run that test, and record “too little data”.
Bootstrap intervalResample the data by stock many times, and see how far the result moves. This plan uses a 90% interval.
Holm correctionA correction that limits false positives when you do several tests at the same time. The false positive rate of all the tests together stays at or below a number that you set first.
Second programA program that a separate Claude agent writes from the written specification only, in a new research folder. It does not see the code of the first program. The main numbers must agree with the first program.
sha256 fingerprintA string calculated from the content of a file. If one character changes, the fingerprint changes. Write the fingerprint into a dated record that you can trust. Later, calculate it again and compare. This proves that the file did not change after that date.
Outside time stampA record that a third party keeps, which proves that a fingerprint existed on a date. Examples: an OpenTimestamps proof, or a snapshot of the public page in the Internet Archive. You can change a commit date. You cannot change an outside time stamp.
Seal and revealAfter a batch, record the sha256 fingerprint of the scores file in a dated note (seal). After that, any change shows. Only after the seal do you see what each chart did next (reveal).
CommitA commit saves a snapshot of the files with a date. Compare the files with this snapshot to find changes.
Current rules (the baseline)Entry: a buy stop 1 tick above the pivot high. Stop: 1 tick below the pattern low. Do not move the stop after the entry. Size: risk 0.2% of the account for each trade when the pullback depth is at least 0.7 daily ATR, 0.1% from 0.4 to 0.7, and no trade below 0.4. Total risk is 3% at most. After an 8% drawdown from the account high, cut the risk for each trade by half. Restore the full risk only at a new account high. Exit: when a close is 3 ATR above the 21 day exponential average, sell half at the next open. From the second session, sell the rest at the next open after a close below the 10 day exponential average. This exit line is the same on every day from the list of Oct 19. From Oct 6 to 16, the line changed with the QQQ state. Hold for 60 sessions at most. 3 new stocks each day at most.
QQQ stateThe trend state of the Nasdaq 100 fund (QQQ) that the scanner sets after each close: green, yellow, amber or red. From the list of Oct 19, the rules do not use it, and the scanner keeps it only as a record. From Oct 6 to 16, it set the exit line (the 10 day exponential average for yellow, the 8 day for the other three). It never decided if you trade.
Tick and slippageA tick is the smallest step of a price, usually $0.01 for US stocks. Slippage is the difference between the order price and the fill price.
Decision logEach session, score the scanner's alerts and write down if you take them. You do not know the result when you write, so this is the only test that memory cannot bias.
Codex and advisorTwo independent AI reviewers. Each registration and this plan need their stamps. Codex reads only, and never reads the data.
ASD STE100A standard for simplified technical English: short sentences, active voice, one meaning for each word.
